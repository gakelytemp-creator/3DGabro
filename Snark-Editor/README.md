# Snark Editor

**მოკლედ.** Snark-ის სხეულების გამოსაცდელი რედაქტორი (ბოლო ვერსია Snark_04): კუბი, ცილინდრი და სფერო; წიბოები სპლაინით, რკალით ან ფასკით; ფიჩერები — გუმბათი, ორმო, ბოსი, კრატერი — ზედაპირებზე, მომრგვალებულ წიბოებზე და სფეროზე, გამეორებით (ბადე, ფიჭა, წრე) და შერწყმის წესებით; ანიმაციაში წიბოები ბოლომდე რბილდება, ფიჩერები ზომას ინარჩუნებს, ზედაპირზე ქაოტურად დახეტიალობენ და ერთმანეთს კვეთენ; ფაილის ჩაწერა და წაკითხვა, ფაილი მეხსიერებაში და ეკრანზე გამოტანა. ერთი HTML ფაილია, ბრაუზერში იხსნება.

Test bench for Snark bodies and the first reference reader and writer of the [Snark standard](../standards/snark/SNARK.md) (draft 0.1). One HTML file: open it in a browser with an internet connection (three.js loads from a CDN).

## What it does

- **Scene:** colours, light and room of EDITOR_35, with Z up as in Cripp: the walls are the coordinate planes, nothing is drawn below zero unless a body goes there, and the room only grows. Labels are off by default.
- **Bodies:** box, cylinder, sphere, with centre, size and rotation in the bottom bar. Selected body yellow.
- **Edge rules:** sharp, round R, chamfer or spline. *Setback* (დახევა) is how far from the edge the rounding starts; *fullness* k shapes the spline: 0 is straight, 0.55 almost a circle, 1 almost sharp. Edge sets: all, vertical, top, bottom, top + vertical for a box; both, top or bottom rim for a cylinder.
- **One pipeline on every change:** objects → SnarkDoc → text → SnarkDoc → recognised bodies → exact surfaces → screen. The status bar says whether writing and reading back gives exactly the same text and objects.
- **Text panel:** the file exactly as it will be written. It can be edited and applied; reading it reports every remark and Boojum (§13 of the standard).
- **Memory panel:** the SnarkDoc as it is held in memory, the recognised bodies, what was drawn and the checks.
- **Display:** precision (chord) 0.5 … 0.001 mm; *Snark reader* or *plain OBJ program (L0)* — what a program that knows only OBJ (Rhino, for example) shows from the same file; patch colours; triangles; edge lines (sharp edges, and faint seams where a rounding starts).
- **Features** (from Snark_02; over edges and on spheres from Snark_03): the second row of the bottom bar. ＋, then a click on a flat face of the selected body or on a sphere, puts a dome there; a click on a feature in the scene selects it. On a sphere the position is longitude λ and latitude φ in degrees, in the sphere's own frame, and the repeat is a circle around a point. Shape: dome (a dimple when h < 0), boss (a pocket when h < 0) or crater, with radius R, height h and fullness (rounding for a boss, rim for a crater); a profile from a file is kept and can be scaled. The small drawing next to the fields is the profile itself. *Repeat*: grid, honeycomb or circle, with pitch, or count and radius; the copies whose collision square fits on the face are made (§9). *Blend* (§8): sum, mix, mix with a crease, union (max:0.5), union of pits (min:0.5), or the rule of the file. The file gets `#@profile`, `#@feature` and `#@pattern` lines, so any Snark reader draws the same shape.
- **Over edges and on spheres** (from Snark_03): a feature near an edge goes over its rounding, like a sticker; a sphere carries features anywhere. Features that stay on flat parts are drawn exactly, as rings that follow the profile. A body with a feature elsewhere is drawn as one grid laid over its whole surface (its "skin"): six charts for a box or a sphere, the side and the two caps for a cylinder; every grid point lies on the exact surface and is moved along the normal by the features' height. r is measured through space (SNARK.md §7.5). Points near a steep wall are moved onto the wall's two sides, so a boss gets a clean wall instead of teeth. The notes say how fine the grid is.
- **Animation** (from Snark_01): the ▶ button in the header. The bodies turn slowly (one turn in 20 s), and every edge setback grows from 1 to 20 mm and back (10 s); a body stops at half its smallest size, so the 40 mm cube becomes a ball. From Snark_03 the features keep their place and their size while the edges soften under them (in Snark_02 they breathed and the edges stopped before them); a body with features is drawn by a coarser skin that moves every 80 ms. The first frame builds the skins; the clock starts after it. From Snark_04 the features also wander (the *chaos* switch in the animation box, on at the start): each copy walks over its body at 3–9 mm/s, smaller ones faster, and its heading turns at a rate made of two sines of unrelated frequencies, so the paths look chaotic, cross each other and go over edges and onto other faces. Where copies overlap, their blend rules combine them (mix:1 when the file says nothing). Switched off, every copy goes back to its place. Only the picture changes: the objects, the file and the history stay as they are. ⛶ full screen; ■ or Esc stops.
- **Files:** open `.snark` or `.obj`; save `.snark` (base mesh with rules); export `.obj` for other programs (final mesh with cards); STL.

## How it was checked

- **`node src/test_core.mjs`** — 85 checks, Node only: rotations round-trip; both example files of the standard are read and written back unchanged on the second pass; objects → file → objects gives the same objects and the same text; every evaluated surface is closed at three precisions, and its volume lies within one chord of the exact formula (Steiner's formula for rounded boxes, Pappus for rounded cylinders); normals do not break at seams; round corners lie on their sphere and chamfer corners are flat; the exported final mesh is closed, has the same volume, and every vertex lies on its card; disagreements follow the trust order of §13. Features (26 of the checks): each shape on a box face is closed, comes back the same from its file, and its volume agrees with the exact 2π∫r·h dr; six features with spline edges; patterns (7 × 7 = 49 copies, honeycomb, circle); two overlapping domes under every rule (with `sum` the volumes add); `max:k` has no step where two borders cross (below); 500 random flat parts with up to 12 holes are triangulated exactly, and the flat part around blended features has no needle triangles; caps of cylinders; a feature that does not fit; `min` on raised features is reported; heights turned over (the animation); example 1 with its boss and 12 dimples; the final export with features. Skins (11 of the checks, from Snark_03): the bare grid of a box lies on the same surface as the exact drawing (the same volume, within 0.05 %, for spline, round and chamfer edges, a mixed set of edges, and setback 20); a dome over the rounding; a feature on the flat part is still drawn exactly; a sphere with a boss at the pole and a ring of domes, also turned, comes back the same from its file; domes over a cylinder's rim; the animation's grid stays closed and faces out at setbacks 1, 6, 12 and 20 mm. Wandering (3 of the checks, from Snark_04): on a box, a cylinder and a turned sphere, a copy's anchor goes to a direction and back exactly; 30 s of wandering at 6–7 mm/s on average, over several faces, with many pairs crossing; the skin stays closed while the edges soften, with fewer than 1 in 10 000 triangles turned against their points' normals (on walls next to chart borders); the same seed gives the same walk, reset puts every copy back, and the body's own copies never move.
- **`node src/test_page.cjs`** — 74 checks in headless Chromium (Playwright), and no page errors: every control, picking in the scene, undo/redo, text editing with a Boojum, both examples, precision 0.001 mm, the L0 view, Save / Export / STL read back, the animation (the setback runs 1 → 20 mm, the cube at 20 mm is a ball within 0.1 mm, Esc leaves everything as it was), features (placing with a click, every shape, the three repeats, the five rules, picking, undo, a copy of a body, every animation frame closed, example 1), skins (the demo closed and written back the same, a feature placed on the sphere by a click and repeated on a circle, picking on the sphere, the cube's boss 4 mm high at setback 1 and at 20), chaos (on at the start; after 10 s the features have wandered and cross, every skin closed, the file unchanged; switched off, every feature is back in its place; Esc), memory view, language and theme. The animation's clock is checked on the light scene of Snark_01: under the software renderer one frame of the Snark_03 demo takes about 0.7 s. `node src/test_page.cjs Snark_03.html` runs the 70 checks that apply to Snark_03, `Snark_02.html` 65, `Snark_01.html` 40, `Snark_00.html` 36.
- All passed on 2026-11-18 in a cloud container (two processor cores) with a software renderer. There the demo (about 850 000 triangles) took 0.8–1.3 s to evaluate; in the animation, building the three skins took about 1.5 s, and moving one skin took 25–35 ms in Snark_04 (30–120 ms in Snark_03, before the walls were searched faster and the bare surface was kept between moves). A frame of the animation moves one skin. The software renderer there needed about 0.7 s to draw a frame, so how smoothly the animation runs on a real graphics card is not known yet. Gakely tried Snark_00 on his own computer the same day.

## Explicit limitations

- One edge rule per body; a profile must be symmetric, with the same setback on both faces.
- Corners follow the product rule of SNARK.md §7.4. Three chamfers meet in a small flat triangle; most CAD programs let the three planes meet at a point instead. Which one Snark wants is open.
- Recognised are only whole boxes (6 plane cards), cylinders (2 planes and a `cyl` card) and spheres. Any other body is shown and kept as its stored mesh; its cards are not used to re-tessellate it.
- Features are point features on box faces, cylinder caps and spheres. Not yet on the side of a cylinder.
- On flat parts a single feature is exact: its rings follow the profile, walls included; where features overlap there, the blended heights are evaluated on a polar grid (at the default precision its spacing is 1/16 of the smallest radius).
- While the features wander (Snark_04), walls that cross a chart border can turn a few triangles against their neighbours (fewer than 1 in 10 000 in the tests): small dark specks on a boss's wall. The animation's grid is coarser, 0.3–0.4 mm.
- On a skin (a feature over a rounding, or on a sphere) the shape is as fine as the grid: about 0.2–0.3 mm for the demo's bodies, at most 120 000 points per body (0.4 mm in the animation). So the precision setting does not reach there, steep walls are drawn as bands between two circles of points, and a few points next to chart borders stay where they are (small teeth may show there). r is measured through space, not along the surface (§7.5, open question 1).
- A sphere's features are written in the frame of its card (`a=`, `u=`, a draft of §5.2); `polar` is the only pattern on a sphere.
- The demo is heavy: about 850 000 triangles, and its final `.obj` export 30 MB, while the `.snark` file that says the same is 12 KB.
- `max:k` and `min:k` (§8.2) as written leave a step of k/4 at the two points where the borders of two features cross. Snark_02 multiplies k′ by s/(s + k/4), s = |h₁| + |h₂|: no step, and almost the same rounding where the features are taller than k. This is a proposal (SNARK.md §18), not yet the standard.
- Curve features (the groove of example 1, pockets of any outline), `fade=`, `grow=`, `region=curve`, curves, creases, levels and seams are kept in the file as written, but not drawn. Recipe `cut` and `int` steps are kept but not evaluated: there is no Boolean engine.
- A recipe step that turns a body differently from its cards cannot be followed yet: the cards are followed and a Boojum is reported.
